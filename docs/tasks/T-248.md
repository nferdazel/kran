## Scope

Decide, per record class, three things: the enforcement mechanism, the parameter key that carries
the period, and whether the period is already settled. Periods that are not settled stay
`NEEDS-COMPLIANCE-REVIEW` and their purge task is not built.

Deliberately out of scope: writing the purge tasks (T-282), the manual anchor copy and its
disclosure (T-249), backup retention on the host (already decided, `docs/COMPLIANCE.md` §6).

## The finding: the mechanism is not uniform

Retention cannot be one sweep with one number, because two of these classes are physically unable
to lose rows.

| Class | Where | Purge possible today | Constraint that decides it | Parameter key (proposed) | Period |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Financial postings | `ledger.journal`, `ledger.journal_line`, module subledgers | No | Immutable by trigger; a correction is a reversing journal, never a delete | none - permanent | Settled: permanent |
| Audit log | `platform.audit_log`, host anchor file | No | `audit_log_no_change_trg` refuses UPDATE/DELETE and `audit_log_no_purge_trg` refuses TRUNCATE; a prefix delete also breaks `platform.audit_verify()` `link_ok` on the first surviving row | `platform.audit.retention_years` | Settled: no purge, at least 10 years (D-206) |
| KYC and identity | `party` | No | Encrypted and masked; period runs from exit | `party.retention_years_after_exit` | Settled: 10 years after exit, no purge path (D-206) |
| Teller sessions and cash | `teller` | No | Cash-control evidence; same weight as the cash it counts | `teller.retention_years` | Settled: no purge, at least 10 years (D-206) |
| Outbox | `platform.outbox` | Yes, by `status` and age | Delivery evidence only, no chain, nothing legal attaches to it | `platform.outbox.retention_days` | Settled: 30 days after `DONE`/`DEAD` (D-206, `NEEDS-COMPLIANCE-REVIEW`) |
| Inbox | `platform.inbox` | Yes, by `received_at` | De-dup rows only; losing one re-delivers an event, nothing worse | `platform.inbox.retention_days` | Settled: 30 days after receipt (D-206, `NEEDS-COMPLIANCE-REVIEW`) |
| Idempotency responses | `platform.idempotency_key` | Yes, sweep exists | D-175: 24h default, 30 days hard cap, may hold member data | `platform.idempotency.ttl_hours` | Settled |
| Sessions and login events | `platform.session` | Yes, `purgeExpiredSessions` | Driven by `session_idle_minutes` / `session_absolute_minutes`, not by retention | none | Settled |
| Backups | Host filesystem | Host cron | 7 daily / 28 weekly / 84 monthly, same host, unencrypted | none | Settled, host not repo |

So five classes needed a signed number (audit, party, teller, outbox, inbox). The other four were
already settled and must not be reopened: financial postings stay permanent, idempotency keeps the
D-175 cap, sessions are governed by expiry not retention, backups are a host policy.

Two things follow from the schema and belong in T-282, not here:

1. **The audit class cannot be purged at all under the current guards.** Purging it would mean
   dropping an immutable trigger and re-baselining the chain, which is a weakening of a guard and
   needs its own decision. The honest options are "keep, and bound growth by an archive-and-rotate
   task with a recorded chain baseline" or "keep with no purge and the growth bound left to that
   archive task". A purge task here is not a smaller change, it is a different one.
2. **`platform.outbox` and `platform.inbox` have no index that supports an age delete.**
   `outbox_dispatch_idx` is `(status, available_at, seq)` and `inbox` is keyed
   `(consumer, event_id)`. An age-based purge scans. T-282 needs an index for each, and that is a
   schema change, which is why T-282 is `full`.

## The signed periods (D-206, 16 Nov 2026)

The five open numbers were settled by the Team A lead on 16 Nov 2026 as a conservative default
until compliance answers; the segment owner replaces them if compliance produces a number, and the
seed changes then.

| Parameter key | Signed value | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| `platform.audit.retention_years` | no purge; at least 10 years (floor) | `NEEDS-COMPLIANCE-REVIEW` |
| `party.retention_years_after_exit` | `10`, counted from exit; no purge path for the pilot | `NEEDS-COMPLIANCE-REVIEW` |
| `teller.retention_years` | no purge; at least 10 years (floor) | `NEEDS-COMPLIANCE-REVIEW` |
| `platform.outbox.retention_days` | `30`, counted from `DONE`/`DEAD`, hard delete | `NEEDS-COMPLIANCE-REVIEW` |
| `platform.inbox.retention_days` | `30`, counted from `received_at`, hard delete | `NEEDS-COMPLIANCE-REVIEW` |

The split is deliberate, because only two of the five can be purged without touching a guard:

- **Evidence classes - audit, party, teller: a 10-year floor, no purge task for the pilot.** They are
  the three that carry legal weight and the three the schema already refuses to delete. The floor is
  UU 8/1997 tentang Dokumen Perusahaan and UU KUP Pasal 28 ayat (11), so audit and teller need no
  number: keeping longer than a minimum is never a violation. Party is the exception in the record
  even though not in the schema: member identity is personal data under UU 27/2022, whose Pasal 74
  transition ended 17 October 2024, so it is written as 10 years after exit rather than an
  open-ended keep. Signing a period shorter than the floor buys nothing and forces the
  guard-dropping question above. Growth is recorded as an accepted operational limit (L-44) and
  T-249's daily anchor is the bound on the audit class.
- **Delivery classes - outbox, inbox: 30 days.** These are the only two with zero evidentiary
  value, no chain and no guard, and `30` matches the D-175 idempotency cap so one sweep can cover
  all three. A wrong number here costs disk, not evidence.

Under this split T-282 shrinks to outbox and inbox purge plus the two indexes, and the audit class
gets a separate archive task that is not a purge.

## Acceptance

1. [x] Every class in the table above has a named mechanism, a parameter key or an explicit "no
   key", and a period status of `settled`. A class with a period and no key, or a key and no
   mechanism, fails this.
2. [x] No period in this task is invented. The five signed values carry `NEEDS-COMPLIANCE-REVIEW`
   and an owner; T-282 encodes them and stays blocked on nothing else.
3. [x] The settled four (financial, idempotency, sessions, backups) are recorded as settled with the
   decision that settled them, so a later task cannot quietly reopen them.
